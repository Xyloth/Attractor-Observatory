# CODEX Campaign 006: Deep Worlds, Perturbation Science, and Evidence Backbone

## Doctrine

Campaign 005 proved that the Observatory can span W1-W13. Campaign 006 must
prove that breadth can become depth. The next build is not a paperwork layer and
not a directory-count exercise. It must deepen representative worlds until they
produce analytically checkable dynamics, perturbation response surfaces,
transferable motif evidence, negative results, and reviewable artifacts.

Evidence hardening remains necessary, but only as the backbone that preserves
scientific work. A bundle, atlas page, or health vector is a failure if it merely
summarizes shallow simulations. The campaign succeeds when deeper world dynamics
teach the Observatory something that the previous alpha worlds could not.

Partial/foundation-slice exit is unavailable. The campaign ends only when every
gate below is green and meaningful, or when a named `BLOCKER-G##.md` explains why
the gate requires Architect or PI intervention.

## Scientific Questions

1. Can selected W1-W13 worlds be pushed from alpha traces to beta-grade
   dynamical instruments with analytic or conservation references?
2. Which motifs survive perturbation, substrate erasure, and implementation
   diversity once the worlds are deep enough to fail in interesting ways?
3. Can the Observatory discover, audit, and draft real candidate motifs without
   promoting them beyond the evidence they have earned?
4. Can negative-space entries become experimental maps of where motifs fail, not
   just labels for cells the search did not hit?
5. Can external reviewers reproduce the deep-world campaign from commands,
   hashes, and generated reports without trusting the builder narrative?

## Non-Toy Bar

The following substitutions fail the campaign:

- a "deepened" world whose state variables move but whose invariants are not
  tested against analytic, conservation, or independently recomputed references;
- a perturbation campaign sampled entirely inside a success basin;
- a motif candidate whose held-out recurrence is identical across every seed,
  world, and decoy;
- a transfer result that passes because substrate identity leaked into the
  feature vector;
- a negative-space entry with no targeted search effort, no alternative
  explanation, or no survival/failure evidence;
- a claim or draft motif that cites exploratory evidence as if it were
  claim-bearing;
- a report generated from constants instead of trace-derived measurements;
- an atlas page hand-written from prose instead of rendered from structured
  evidence;
- an Instrument Health scalar that hides a failing vector component;
- a campaign-stack reproducer that checks only that commands run.

## Pillar 1: Beta-Grade World Upgrades

G1. W4 Morphogenesis beta implements at least two coupled morphogen fields, local
cell rules, lineage graph, tissue growth, differentiation, segmentation/branching
failure modes, and an energy/material budget checked at every step.

G2. W4 includes analytic or independently recomputed sanity checks: diffusion
mass behavior, lineage acyclicity, tissue-boundary consistency, and at least one
known failure regime where segmentation or branching does not emerge.

G3. W6 Ecosystem beta implements at least four species or guilds, multiple
resources, trophic interactions, niche construction, disturbance/recovery, and
nonnegative stoichiometric resource accounting.

G4. W6 includes ecological regime references: extinction, coexistence,
oscillation, and recovery-after-disturbance regimes are all represented with
generated traces and classified without constants.

G5. W7 Swarm beta implements agent motion, local sensing, communication,
environmental marker writing/decay, marker reading, obstacle or gradient
navigation, and communication ablations.

G6. W7 includes externalized-memory causality checks: marker-write, marker-read,
and marker-removed runs must distinguish actual environmental memory from mere
movement correlation.

G7. W9 Origins Chemistry beta implements mineral-surface adsorption, catalysis,
wet/dry or gradient cycling, side reactions, surface saturation, and connection
to RAF/closure analysis.

G8. W9 includes mass/surface conservation, closure-depth telemetry, and at least
one poisoned-surface negative control where apparent closure fails.

G9. W11 Quasispecies beta implements mutation matrix dynamics, stochastic
lineages, master sequence tracking, parasitic variants, and error-threshold
transition detection.

G10. W11 includes an analytic Eigen-style reference or deterministic mutation
matrix comparison, with error-threshold location error within a declared
tolerance.

G11. W12 Symbiogenesis beta implements host/symbiont conflict, resource exchange,
engulfment, vertical transmission, failed integration, lineage inheritance, and
alignment dynamics.

G12. W12 includes mass/resource accounting, lineage consistency, and at least
two regimes: stable integration and collapse/cheating.

G13. W13 Multiscale beta implements explicit micro, meso, and macro layers with
upscale/downscale flux, cross-scale feedback, nested composition, and scale
separation failure.

G14. W13 includes budget accounting across layers, lag/phase telemetry, and
cross-scale composition tests that distinguish genuine coupling from correlated
parallel movement.

G15. Each beta world emits traces with nontrivial events, invariant checks,
parameter records, mode tags, deterministic/replayable seed behavior, and at
least one documented failure regime.

## Pillar 3: Perturbation Science and Basin Maps

world and at least three perturbation axes per world.

G22. Every perturbation map must include success, failure, and ambiguous regions.
Maps entirely inside one class fail.

G23. Compute basin width with bootstrap confidence intervals for at least eight
motif/world pairs. CI width must be <= 0.20 where a basin boundary is claimed.

G24. Compute response curves for damage, resource limitation, mutation pressure,
coupling strength, and signal/noise where those axes apply.

G25. Add adversarial perturbation targets that are designed to create false
positives for closure, boundary, memory, lineage, and multiscale composition.

G26. Record negative results as structured failure artifacts with failure type:
structural, informative, or boundary.

G27. Add a perturbation dashboard report that ranks motifs by robustness vector,
not by a single score.

## Builder Expansion Authority

The builder may deepen any beta world beyond the named minimum where it improves
scientific value, especially by adding independent references, adversarial
regimes, better invariants, or better failure artifacts. The builder may add a
compact HTML Atlas viewer, JSON schema validation for application-layer bundles,
extra detector prototypes, richer red-team decoys, and reviewer README
generation.

The builder may not change the trace schema, mode-tag vocabulary, claim-status
vocabulary, license classes, retention classes, or motif registry contract inside
this campaign. If implementation reveals those contracts are insufficient, write
a named blocker/proposal instead of silently changing them.
